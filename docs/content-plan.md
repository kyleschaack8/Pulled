# Card Market Movers — 4-Week Content Plan

Nov 1, 2026 · @Kyle Schaack

## Goal

Find out in 4 weeks whether collectors care about "why did my card move" before spending money on an app. Post a weekly market recap on NBA rookie and star cards, tie each price move to a game, injury or trade, and measure whether people follow and ask for more.

Success by the end of week 4 looks like:

- 300+ followers across channels, or 100+ newsletter subscribers
- At least 5 unprompted "what about my card / player X?" requests per week
- One post that clearly outperforms the rest, showing which format resonates

## Setup (before week 1)

Pick a narrow watchlist and one place to keep the numbers; everything else builds on that.

| Item | Choice | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Niche | NBA: top 15 rookies + 10 stars | Rookies move most on performance |
| Cards per player | 1–2 benchmark cards | e.g. flagship base rookie, PSA 10 and raw |
| Price source | eBay sold listings, Card Ladder free tier | Use sold prices only, never asking prices |
| Stats + injuries | NBA.com, Basketball Reference, team injury reports | Free |
| Tracking | One spreadsheet: player, card, price each Sunday, % change, reason | This sheet becomes your first dataset |
| Channels | TikTok or Instagram Reels + X; free newsletter (Beehiiv or Substack) | Same content, cut per channel |
| Name + handle | Pulled (working name) | Check pulled.app and @pulled / @pulledcards; grab the same handle on every channel |

## Weekly post formats

Three repeatable formats, each one a mini version of a feature the app would have.

| Format | When | What it shows | App feature it tests |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Market Movers | Monday | Top 5 risers and fallers of the week, one-line reason each | "Why it moved" |
| Price vs. Performance | Wednesday | One player's card price chart with game markers on it | Card page overlay |
| What to Watch | Friday | Upcoming games, injury returns, award races that could move prices | Catalyst calendar |

End every post with the same ask: "Comment a player and I'll check his cards next week." Those replies are your demand signal.

## Week by week

**Week 1 — Baseline and first posts**

- [ ] Build the tracking sheet and record starting prices for all 25 players
- [ ] Set up channels and newsletter with the same handle
- [ ] Post one intro: "Every week I track how NBA games move card prices"
- [ ] Publish the first Market Movers post (even if moves are small)

**Week 2 — Full cadence**

- [ ] All three formats: Monday, Wednesday, Friday
- [ ] First Price vs. Performance chart, picking the player with the biggest game
- [ ] Reply to every comment within 24 hours

**Week 3 — Follow the demand**

- [ ] Answer the most-requested player from comments as its own post
- [ ] Post in 2–3 card communities (Reddit, Facebook groups) where self-promotion is allowed
- [ ] Note which format gets the most saves, shares and follows

**Week 4 — Test willingness to pay**

- [ ] Double down on the best format
- [ ] Post a waitlist link: "Building an app that does this for your whole collection"
- [ ] Ask 5–10 engaged followers what they would pay for alerts on their own cards

## Weekly routine (about 5–6 hours)

Built to fit around a full-time job.

| Day | Task | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sunday evening | Update prices in the sheet, flag the biggest moves | 1.5 h |
| Sunday evening | Match each big move to a game, injury or news item | 1 h |
| Monday | Record and post Market Movers | 45 min |
| Wednesday | Make one chart, post Price vs. Performance | 1 h |
| Friday | Post What to Watch | 45 min |
| Daily, 10 min | Reply to comments, log player requests | \~1 h/week |

## Decision at the end of week 4

Go, adjust, or stop, based on the numbers in the Goal section.

- **Go:** goals met or close, and people asked about their own cards. Next step is a simple web version with player pages and price charts, no scanner yet.
- **Adjust:** some engagement but weak growth. Try a different niche (MLB prospects, NFL rookies) or channel for 4 more weeks.
- **Stop:** little engagement after 12 posts. The pain point may not be strong enough; move on with what you learned.
